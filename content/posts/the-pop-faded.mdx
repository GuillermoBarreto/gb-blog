I checked the price this morning before I did anything else. Anthropic closed its first day at $97.17. That's up 17% from the $83 IPO price, which sounds great until you see how the day actually went.

The stock opened at $111.50, 34% above the offer price, and touched $115.10 before noon. Then it started sliding, and it was below $100 within 17 minutes of the first trade. It spent the afternoon between about $93 and $98 and closed at $97.17. The opening gain was $28.50 a share. The close kept $14.17 of it. Almost exactly half.

About 857 million shares changed hands, worth roughly $83 billion at the close. On the shares actually outstanding, the company ended the day worth about $1.28 trillion. Fully diluted, counting everything that could become a share, it's closer to $1.8 trillion. Either way, the biggest IPO on record. And this morning in pre-market trading it was down again, around $86.62 when I looked, about 11% below the close.

I don't own the stock, and I don't own Nvidia either. I'm not telling you to buy or sell anything. I have no idea where this goes next. What I know is the shape of the day. The market paid $111.50 for the story in the morning and $97.17 for it by the afternoon. That's not a crash. It's a haircut. And it happened on the same day the rest of the industry kept building.

Because while Anthropic's shares were sliding, Nvidia and Microsoft were on stage at Microsoft Ignite announcing that Vera Rubin NVL72 systems are running in Azure data centers. Rubin is Nvidia's newest AI platform, the hardware that trains and runs the biggest models. Microsoft says it was the first cloud to bring one up for validation back in March, and it got the first production systems in August. Now customers can try them through Azure virtual machines.

But it's a preview. Neither company gave a virtual machine series name, a list of regions, pricing, or a date for general availability. You can try it. You can't plan a budget or a rollout around it yet. Microsoft's own Ignite demo used one compute node with four Rubin GPUs to run a 397-billion-parameter model workflow. Confidential computing on Rubin is also a technology preview, starting with bare-metal instances, and the confidential virtual machines are still in development.

The more usable part is in Microsoft Foundry, where developers customize and deploy models. Programmed Training is in public preview: developers hand Foundry their own Python training recipes while Foundry runs them on serverless GPUs. Foundry Managed Compute is generally available now, for deploying open, partner, and custom model weights, including Nvidia's Nemotron 3.5 open models. The proof point Nvidia put forward is EY, which used Programmed Training to adapt Nemotron models to classify corporate purchasing records. Nvidia says accuracy on EY's own evaluation dataset went up 55% and cost dropped 15 times compared with proprietary frontier models. Those are vendor-reported numbers on one customer's dataset, so take them as a claim, not a benchmark.

There's more in the same announcement. Azure Local now supports Nemotron 3.5 models and Nvidia's RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell GPUs for on-premises AI. Jetson Thor kits are in public preview for edge and robotics work. On Windows PCs, RTX Spark machines can run large language models locally, shown as Ignite demos rather than a finished product. And in the MLPerf Training benchmark, Azure scaled to 10,240 Nvidia GPUs.

I've been writing about versions of the same pattern all month. A prepayment is not a lease. A merge is not a review. A promise is not a result. Today it showed up twice, in opposite directions. The market gave Anthropic a price, and the price was a check. The builders gave us a preview, and a preview is a promise. The price you can argue with. The preview you just have to wait on.

I'm a WGU software engineering student. I work IT support during the day and write code at night. I don't run a data center and I've never priced a cloud contract. But I know what a preview means in my own work. It means the thing exists, it runs, and it isn't finished yet.

The durable skill here is the same one it always is. Read the filing. Read the numbers. A closing price tells you what the market thought today. A preview with no price and no date tells you the build is still ahead of the check.

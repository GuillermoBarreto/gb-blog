Here's something I wish someone told me earlier: the tutorials teach you *how* to code, but they don't teach you *where the industry is going*. And that second part? It matters more than I thought.

## What changed my mind

A while back I was deep in a tutorial hole — just grinding through lessons, head down. Then in a conversation about jobs, someone mentioned AI infrastructure and data center power constraints, and I had nothing to say. Not because I'm not technical enough. Because I hadn't been paying attention to the world outside my editor.

That stung a little. So I started reading tech news every morning with coffee. Fifteen minutes. Nothing crazy.

## It's not about memorizing headlines

Nobody's going to quiz you on funding rounds. The value is pattern recognition. After a few weeks you start noticing: which tools keep showing up in real job posts, which problems companies keep throwing money at, which skills keep appearing in the "requirements" section.

That context quietly changes your decisions. You stop learning things at random and start learning things *on purpose*.

## The habit that actually stuck

I tried the "read everything" approach. Lasted three days. Here's what actually works for me:

```js
// The junior dev news habit (the version I kept)
const habit = {
  time: "15 minutes, with coffee",
  sources: ["one aggregator", "one community", "one deep dive"],
  output: "one sentence: what changed, and why it matters to me",
};

console.log("Consistency beats intensity:", habit.time);
```

Fifteen minutes. One sentence in my own words. That's it. Some days the sentence is "I have no idea what this means yet" — and that's fine, that's a starting point, not a failure.

## The real payoff

In interviews, the devs who stand out aren't just the ones who can write the algorithm. They're the ones who can say *why* something matters. "I read that companies are hitting power limits on AI training, so I started learning about infrastructure" — that's a different conversation than "I know Python."

I'm still early in this. But every article is a rep. Start today, fifteen minutes, coffee optional but recommended.

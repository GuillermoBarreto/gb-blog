When you're learning to code, it's tempting to put your head down and focus only on tutorials. But the tech industry moves fast, and understanding *where it's moving* is a skill in itself.

## It's not about the headlines

Nobody needs you to recite funding rounds. The value is in pattern recognition: which tools are gaining real adoption, which problems keep coming up, which skills employers keep asking for. Over time, that context shapes better decisions about what to learn next.

## A simple habit

You don't need an hour a day. Fifteen minutes with one good source beats doomscrolling five. A practical loop:

```js
// The junior dev news habit
const habit = {
  time: "15 minutes",
  frequency: "daily",
  sources: ["one aggregator", "one community", "one deep dive"],
  output: "one sentence: what changed, and why it matters",
};

console.log("Consistency beats intensity:", habit.frequency);
```

## The real payoff

In interviews and in teams, the developers who stand out aren't just the ones who know syntax — they're the ones who understand context. They can say *why* a technology matters, not just *how* it works.

That context is built one article at a time. Start today.
